How the model works
  • The loop carries a slurry of dielectric oil with 20% paraffin beads by mass, at 20 g/s. One lap takes 20 seconds.
  • The sealed container holds 4 kg of paraffin around a copper coil, with fins. Paraffin stores 200 kJ/kg when it melts.
  • The finned coil moves 150 W of heat between slurry and wax for every 1 °C between them. The chimney lets out 4 W per 1 °C the wax is above the outside air.
  • At full speed the dry cooler moves 50 W per 1 °C. Its fan slows to send slurry back about 6 °C below the melting point.
  • The chip runs 0.025 °C/W above the slurry under it and slows down above 85 °C.
  • Cooling tower water: heat ÷ 2,260 kJ/kg. Not modelled: supercooling, pipe losses.
