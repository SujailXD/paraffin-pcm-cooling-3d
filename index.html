How the loop works

The fluid carries tiny beads of paraffin wax, each sealed in a plastic shell. Melting a bead takes a lot of heat, so the fluid can carry heat away without getting much hotter. Pick a step to look closer.

How the model works
  • Fluid: dielectric oil carrying 20% paraffin beads by mass, flowing at 1 kg/s. One lap of the loop takes 40 seconds.
  • Wax stores 200 kJ/kg when it melts and melts over a 3 °C range. Specific heat of the slurry is 1.9 kJ/kg·K.
  • Each GPU sits 0.03 °C/W hotter than the fluid under it, so a 700 W chip runs 21 °C above the fluid. Chips slow down above 85 °C.
  • At full fan speed the dry cooler moves 4 kW of heat for every 1 °C the fluid is warmer than the outside air. The fans slow down to send the slurry back about 6 °C below the wax’s melting point, so the melting wax does most of the work.
  • Cooling tower water: heat ÷ 2,260 kJ/kg, the energy it takes to evaporate 1 kg of water.
  • Not modelled: beads freezing a few degrees late (supercooling), pump power, heat lost from pipes.
